# Sources for the clergy events

These are the real cases behind the `clergy-*` pieces in `src/events2.js`. They were written from memory of the standard accounts and have not been re-checked against the originals, so treat dates and figures as leads to verify before they are quoted anywhere outside the game. This file should be folded into `docs/SOURCES.md` when that lands on this branch.

- **Richard Brothers** (`clergy-brothers`). Former naval lieutenant, self-styled Prince of the Hebrews. *A Revealed Knowledge of the Prophecies and Times* (1794). Foretold the destruction of London on the King's birthday, 4 June 1795. Arrested March 1795 and confined as a lunatic. Nathaniel Brassey Halhed, M.P., defended him in the Commons. The Rector of Wyvern who preaches it is invented.
- **Clergy and the Volunteers** (`clergy-arms`). In April 1798 the bishops, meeting at Lambeth, resolved that clergy should not bear arms, and the Archbishop circulated it. Many parsons joined or raised corps regardless.
- **Horne Tooke and Old Sarum** (`clergy-tooke`). John Horne Tooke, in orders since 1760, was returned for Old Sarum in 1801. Parliament passed the Clergy Disqualification Act (41 Geo. III c. 63) to bar clergy from the Commons thereafter.
- **The non-residence informations** (`clergy-informer`). Under 21 Hen. VIII c. 13 a non-resident incumbent forfeited £10 a month, half to the informer. Around 1799 to 1801 an attorney's clerk brought several hundred such actions. Parliament suspended the suits and Sir William Scott's Clergy Residence Act (43 Geo. III c. 84, 1803) gave bishops power to license absence. Fenny Sutterby is invented.
- **Dr Trusler's sermons** (`clergy-trusler`). The Rev. John Trusler sold sermons printed in a script type imitating handwriting, from 1769, so that a preacher might seem to read his own manuscript.
- **Tithe in kind** (`clergy-tithe`). The law required a farmer to set out the tithe and give notice; farmers' reprisals by awkward hours and awkward lots are well attested in the tithe cases and in Woodforde's and Skinner's diaries.
- **The Blagdon controversy** (`clergy-school`). 1800 to 1803. Thomas Bere, curate of Blagdon, accused Hannah More's Mendip schools of Methodism. Some twenty-three pamphlets followed.
- **Kemp v. Wickes** (`clergy-burial`). The Rev. John Wight Wickes, rector of Wardley with Belton, Rutland, refused in 1808 to bury a child baptised by a Dissenting minister. Sir John Nicholl in the Court of Arches (December 1809) held lay and Dissenting baptism valid and suspended him for three months.
- **Mass confirmations** (`clergy-bishop`). Berkshire lay in the diocese of Salisbury. Bishops confirmed hundreds at a sitting, by railfuls, on visitations years apart.
- **Hunting parsons** (`clergy-hunting`). A general type of the period. The funeral from the saddle is invented in the manner of the stock anecdotes.
- **Joanna Southcott** (`clergy-southcott`). Announced in 1814, at sixty-four, that she would bear Shiloh. Followers were "sealed" with signed papers. A satinwood crib was made by Seddons of Aldersgate Street, reportedly for £200, and a silver pap-spoon was among the gifts. Several Anglican clergy followed her, the Rev. Thomas Foley of Oldswinford among them. She died 27 December 1814; the post-mortem found no child.
