# Corpus

The period in its own words: the ear to be trained is trained here. Everything in this folder is out of copyright.

**State of play.** Passages of a few pages each are here, cut from the Internet Archive's scans, each with its source at the head. Whole books are not kept in the repo. `tools/fetch-corpus.sh` pulls the full texts into `corpus/full/`, which git ignores.

In the folder now: Woodforde (Christmas 1788, Christmas 1789, and short quotations to 1802), Mavor on Berkshire labour (1809), Eden on Reading (1795), Cobbett riding into Newbury, a letter of Jane Austen's, two letters from *Humphry Clinker*, and the opening of *Castle Rackrent*.

Facts worth having by heart: Eden found common labourers at Reading on 9s. a week in September 1795, with the quartern loaf at 11½d. Mavor in 1809 gives a day labourer 9s. to 12s.

## What to read for what

| For | Read |
|---|---|
| The narrator's irony and the weight of a sentence | Austen, *Emma* and *Persuasion*; her letters for the same mind off duty |
| Country anger, prices, soil, and plain force | Cobbett, *Rural Rides* (1820s, but the voice was formed in our years) |
| A steward telling his master's story | Edgeworth, *Castle Rackrent* (1800). Crabbe's cousin in Ireland |
| Young women writing letters about dreadful men | Burney, *Evelina* |
| Squires, justices, inns, the road | Fielding, *Tom Jones*; Smollett, *Humphry Clinker*, which is all letters, each in its own hand |
| Digression and the dash used by a master (admire, do not copy) | Sterne, *Tristram Shandy* |
| A parish through the seasons | Gilbert White, *Selborne*; Mitford, *Our Village* (Berkshire, three miles from Reading) |
| Low words | Grose, *Dictionary of the Vulgar Tongue*, 1811 |
| The pocket-book | Parson Woodforde's diary |
| What things cost in Berkshire | Mavor, *General View of the Agriculture of Berkshire* (1809); Eden, *The State of the Poor* (1797); Davies, *The Case of Labourers in Husbandry* (1795), not yet found |

Still wanted: a run of the *Reading Mercury* or any provincial paper of the 1790s, the *Gentleman's Magazine* obituaries, Gillray's caption texts, and a real estate letter-book. The Berkshire Record Office has all of these. Photographs of a few pages would be worth more than everything above.
