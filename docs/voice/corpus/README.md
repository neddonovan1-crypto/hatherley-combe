# Corpus

The period in its own words: the ear to be trained is trained here. Everything in this folder is out of copyright.

**State of play.** Project Gutenberg was down for maintenance on the day this was set up, so the texts are not yet here. Run `tools/fetch-corpus.sh` from the repo root when it is back. The script prints each book's title as it lands, so a wrong number shows itself.

## What to read for what

| For | Read |
|---|---|
| The narrator's irony and the weight of a sentence | Austen, *Emma* and *Persuasion*; her letters for the same mind off duty |
| Country anger, prices, soil, and plain force | Cobbett, *Rural Rides* (1820s, but the voice was formed in our years) |
| A steward telling his master's story | Edgeworth, *Castle Rackrent* (1800). Crabbe's cousin in Ireland |
| Young women writing letters about dreadful men | Burney, *Evelina* |
| Squires, justices, inns, the road | Fielding, *Tom Jones*; Smollett, *Humphry Clinker*, which is all letters, each in its own hand |
| Digression and the dash used by a master (admire, do not copy) | Sterne, *Tristram Shandy* |
| A parish through the seasons | Gilbert White, *Selborne*; Mitford, *Our Village* (Berkshire, three miles from Reading) |
| Low words | Grose, *Dictionary of the Vulgar Tongue*, 1811 |
| The pocket-book | Parson Woodforde's diary. Not on Gutenberg. Extracts at parsonwoodforde.org.uk; fragments below |

Still wanted and not on Gutenberg in usable form: a run of the *Reading Mercury* or any provincial paper of the 1790s, the *Gentleman's Magazine* obituaries, Gillray's caption texts, and a real estate letter-book. The Berkshire Record Office has all of these. Photographs of a few pages would be worth more than everything above.

## Woodforde, fragments

Taken from the Parson Woodforde Society's transcriptions, for the shape of an entry and the way he rules his sums.

- 4 June 1776: "Gave the old Man that drew it however - 0 : 2 : 6" (a tooth)
- 21 May 1778: "She has a pension from the Crown now of 18 - 5 - 0"
- 25 January 1787: "I recd. of the Officers for marrying them - 0 - 10 - 6." and then, "It is very disagreeable to me to marry such Persons."
- 4 August 1789, on seeing the King: "The King looked very red and is very tall & erect"
- 6 August 1801, on the invasion papers: "It hurried and affected me a great deal indeed"
- 17 October 1802, the last entry: "Very weak this Morning, scarce able to put on my Cloaths" and "Dinner today Rost Beef &c."

Note what he does. The sum comes at the end of the line. The feeling, when there is one, gets a sentence to itself and no adjective stronger than "disagreeable". The last thing he ever wrote down was dinner.
