# James Woodforde, diary: short quotations, 1776 to 1802

Sources: The Diary of a Country Parson, ed. Beresford, vol. V (1931), archive.org/details/diaryofcountrypa0005wood, and the Parson Woodforde Society's transcriptions at parsonwoodforde.org.uk. Volume V is in American copyright as an edition until January 2027, so only brief quotations are kept here. Longer runs from volume III are in the two neighbouring files.

---

December 1797, after his tithe audit dinner: "Legs of Mutton and Capers, a very fine Surloin of Beef rosted, Salt Fish with plenty of plumb Puddings &c. Punch. Port Wine & strong Beer. A very pleasing Tithe Frolic indeed, and all left me by ten in the Evening, very well pleased with their Frolic. Every thing went off very harmonious & no Noise."

Decmbr. 17, 1797: "Dinner to day, Shoulder of Veal rosted &c. Very windy all the Day, obliged to be in the Parlour as our Study smoaked so very much. Wind W.N.W."

Decbr. 18, 1797: "To a Fisherman for a Pike paid this Morn' 0. 1. 6."

4 June 1776, a tooth: "Gave the old Man that drew it however - 0 : 2 : 6"

25 January 1787: "I recd. of the Officers for marrying them - 0 - 10 - 6." "It is very disagreeable to me to marry such Persons."

6 August 1801, on the invasion papers: "It hurried and affected me a great deal indeed"

17 October 1802, the last entry: "Very weak this Morning, scarce able to put on my Cloaths" and "Dinner today Rost Beef &c."
